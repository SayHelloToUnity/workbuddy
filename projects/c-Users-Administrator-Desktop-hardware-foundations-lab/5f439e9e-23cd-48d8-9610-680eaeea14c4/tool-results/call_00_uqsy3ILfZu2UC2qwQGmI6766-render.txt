Why two slopes can cancel The adjustment voltage between R and V− is about 64 mV at 25 °C and is proportional to absolute temperature. In the plain circuit the internal bias current makes the effective setting voltage about 67.7 mV. The nominal room-temperature estimate is 67.7 mV / 68 Ω ≈ 0.996 mA. Around 25 °C, a proportional-to-absolute-temperature current changes by about 1 / 298.15, or +0.335%, per degree Celsius. A 20 °C rise therefore adds about 6.7% to the plain current, before smaller circuit effects. With the diode fitted, approximate total current as the sum of the bias-adjusted first branch and the second resistor branch. At 25 °C the first is 67.7 mV / 133 Ω ≈ 0.509 mA.
